MM03

Medication Administration Policy and Procedure

v4.0
Action neededHigh impact Approved 20 Aug 20268 acknowledgements outstandingEasy Read v2 out of date
Medications Management · Supported Living, Domiciliary

Review sheet

Owner: Deputy Manager. Approved by Registered Manager, 20 August 2026. Next scheduled review 14 February 2027. Reviewed six-monthly or on regulatory change, whichever is sooner.

Aligned to CQC Single Assessment Framework — Safe (Medicines optimisation), Effective, Responsive.

Purpose

To set out how Prosperity Care & Wellbeing supports people to take their medication safely, in a way that respects their choices and keeps decision-making with the person wherever they are able to make it.

Scope

Applies to all staff working in Supported Living (Cheltenham) and Domiciliary Care (Gloucestershire), including bank and agency workers. Not in scope for Wellbeing Outreach (Stroud), which does not handle medication.

4. Policy

4.1Staff must not administer any medication unless they have completed the Safe Handling of Medicines training and been assessed as competent within the preceding twelve months.

4.4Where a person has been assessed as able to self-administer, staff must not remove that responsibility without a documented reassessment and the person's involvement in that decision.

4.6Covert administration may only be used where a documented best-interests decision exists, recorded under CR46 Mental Capacity Act 2005, agreed with the GP and pharmacist, and reviewed every three months.

4.7The person's Easy Read medication summary must be updated within five working days of any change to 4.6.

5. Procedure

5.2A person may refuse their medication. Refusal must be respected, recorded, and reported to the prescriber. It must never be disguised, forced or given without the person's knowledge outside of clause 4.6.

5.5Where a person wishes to change how, when or where their medication is given, staff must record the request and raise it at the next review rather than declining it at the point of care.

5.9Any medication error, near-miss or refusal must be recorded on the eMAR within the same shift and escalated to the Deputy Manager without delay.

Definitions

  • Covert administration — giving medication disguised in food or drink without the person's knowledge.
  • Self-administration — where the person manages and takes their own medication, with or without prompting.
  • Time-critical medication — medication that must be given within 30 minutes of the prescribed time.

Key facts — staff

Staff providing this service should be aware of the following:

  • You must be trained and assessed as competent before administering any medication
  • A person can refuse. Record it, respect it, tell the prescriber. Never persuade or disguise
  • Covert administration needs a documented best-interests decision, not just GP agreement
  • If someone asks to change how their medication is given, record the request — don't decline it on the spot
  • Errors and near-misses go on the eMAR the same shift and to the Deputy Manager immediately
